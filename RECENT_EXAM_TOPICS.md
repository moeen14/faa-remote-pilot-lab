# Recently reported Part 107 (UAG) exam topics

This is a **living log**, not a leaked question bank. FAA test questions are
proprietary and test-takers agree not to reproduce them, so this file never
records verbatim questions. Instead it tracks two things over time:

1. **Official FAA changes** to the test itself (blueprint weight, format, new
   task codes) — these come straight from FAA's own published bulletins.
2. **Recurring topics and mistake patterns** that test-prep sites and
   test-takers report seeing on real, recent exams — cited to the source that
   reported them.

Ask an AI assistant with web access to revisit this file periodically (e.g.
"check for updates to RECENT_EXAM_TOPICS.md") and append new dated entries
below rather than rewriting history. Keep entries dated and sourced so the
log stays auditable.

## Official FAA test-format changes

### 2025-09-29 — UAG test blueprint reweighted; old ACS percentages obsolete

Per the FAA Airman Testing Community Advisory (August 2025) and the PSI
*UAG Information Bulletin*, the knowledge areas tested did **not** change,
but how heavily each is weighted changed dramatically, based on a 2025
industry job-task analysis:

| Knowledge area (ACS area) | Old ACS weight | **New weight (since 2025‑09‑29)** |
|---|---|---|
| I. Regulations | 15–25% | **48%** |
| II. Airspace Classification & Operating Requirements | 15–25% | **20%** |
| III. Weather | 11–16% | **5%** |
| IV. Loading and Performance | 7–11% | **2%** |
| V. Operations (radio comms, airport ops, emergencies, CRM, ADM, physiology, maintenance) | 35–45% | **25%** |

Format: 60 scored questions **plus 5 unscored "validation" questions** FAA
uses to pretest future items (you cannot tell which 5 are unscored), 120
minutes, 70% to pass, 14-day wait to retest after a fail, no instructor
endorsement required to sit the test or a retest.

**Practical implication for study:** regulations (14 CFR Part 107 text
itself, operating limitations, waivers, Remote ID, and especially
Operations Over People) now dominate the exam. Do not neglect weather and
loading questions — they are still tested and still operationally real —
but budget study time roughly in proportion to the table above.

Sources: FAA ATCA, August 2025 edition (faa.gov/training_testing/testing);
PSI *UAG Information Bulletin*, effective 2025‑09‑29
(media.psiexams.com/faa/UAG_Information_Bulletin.pdf).

### 2026-10-26 (scheduled) — Exam adds live/dynamic chart images

Starting **October 26, 2026**, the UAG (and Instrument-Airplane) knowledge
tests begin including image-based questions drawn from **current, dated
Sectional Charts, Instrument Approach Charts, and Low Altitude Enroute
Charts** — not the fixed, numbered figures in the printed testing
supplement (FAA-CT-8080-2H). FAA's own bulletin gives two example
question types: a Washington sectional excerpt (dated 12 Jun–7 Aug 2025)
asking about the airspace class and required ATC authorization near
Millville Muni (MIV), and a Las Vegas sectional excerpt asking about the
correct CTAF to use at Grand Canyon National Park (GCN) when the tower is
closed. The printed supplement will still be available at test centers for
now, but FAA has not committed to a date for retiring it.

**Practical implication:** memorizing "Figure 23" from the supplement is
becoming less useful. What matters going forward is **reading the sectional
chart legend and symbology itself** — airspace class boundaries and floor/
ceiling numbers, CTAF/UNICOM frequency boxes, airport symbol conventions,
obstruction height pairs (AGL/MSL), special-use airspace shapes. This
site's chart-reading flashcards are built around teaching the legend, not
one memorized excerpt.

Source: FAA Airman Testing Community Advisory, "September 2026 Special
Edition" (faa.gov/training_testing/testing), "Graphics Conversion Project."

### FAA's own "frequently missed" statistic: Operations Over People (Declaration of Compliance)

FAA publishes a quarterly *Frequently Missed Knowledge Test ACS Codes*
report. For FY2025 Q2 (July 2024–June 2025), the only UAG-specific code
that cleared FAA's reporting threshold (≥100 administrations, ≥70% missed)
was **UA.I.E.K13 — the Declaration of Compliance for Operations Over
People**, missed **73.33%** of the time. This site gives that topic a
dedicated flashcard cluster (see the "Operations over people" topic) for
exactly this reason.

Source: FAA, *Frequently Missed ACS Codes, FY 2025 2nd Quarter*
(faa.gov/data_research/aviation_data_statistics/test_statistics).

## Recurring topics reported by test-takers and prep sites (not verbatim questions)

*Reddit (r/Part107, r/drones) could not be searched directly by the
research tool that compiled this list; the entries below come from public
test-prep blog posts that say they incorporate test-taker feedback. Treat
them as "industry-reported," not FAA-official, unless a source above says
otherwise.*

- **2026-08** — The Drone Girl, *"The Part 107 test changed twice. Here's
  what you actually need to study now"* — advises reading the Part 107
  regulatory text directly, building transferable sectional-chart-reading
  skill rather than memorizing figures, and treating §107.49's preflight
  check as a decision framework (weather / airspace / equipment / hazards).
  (thedronegirl.com/2026/08/13/part-107-changes/)

- **Pilot Institute**, *"The 12 Most Missed Part 107 Knowledge Test
  Questions"* — recurring missed-topic themes: confusing a cloud ceiling
  with the 400 ft AGL altitude limit (remember the +500 ft buffer near
  clouds); the standard pressure lapse rate (~1 inHg per 1,000 ft); Maximum
  Elevation Figures (MEF) on sectional charts always being in MSL; Military
  Training Route numbering (4-digit routes fly below 1,500 ft AGL, 3-digit
  routes can go above); converting a runway number to a magnetic heading;
  confusing the drone registration 14-day address-change deadline with
  certificate renewal; AGL/MSL conversion for the 400-ft-above-a-structure
  rule; identifying a non-towered airport's CTAF symbol (magenta) on a
  sectional; converting the 100 mph groundspeed limit to ~87 knots; and
  remembering that the FAA does not prescribe a specific repair protocol
  for a damaged drone — that is the manufacturer's call.
  (pilotinstitute.com/part-107-questions/)

- **2025** — John Peltier (jmpeltier.com), *"New Part 107 Remote Pilot
  Questions for 2025"* — based on direct test-taker feedback, flagged five
  newly emphasized topics: civil vs. military vs. public-aircraft
  jurisdiction under Part 107; operating from a moving vehicle in a
  sparsely populated area; Operations-Over-People category requirements by
  aircraft type; runway safety-area markings and hold-short procedures; and
  density-altitude effects on fixed-wing (not just multirotor) performance.
  (jmpeltier.com/new-part-107-remote-pilot-questions-2025/)

- **General 2025–2026 prep-industry consensus** (multiple blogs: Drone U,
  Drone Pilot Ground School, RedRavenUAS, TestPrepNation, DroneBundle) —
  the perennially hardest areas remain weather decoding (METAR/TAF,
  density altitude, temperature inversions, thunderstorm/microburst
  hazards) and sectional chart reading (lat/long, airspace class
  boundaries, MEFs) — even though weather now carries only 5% of the test
  by the 2025 reweighting, and straight regulatory recall (operating
  limitations, registration, waivers, Remote ID, Operations Over People
  categories), which is now nearly half the exam.

## How to extend this file

When asked to refresh this log, an AI assistant with web access should:

1. Check faa.gov/training_testing/testing for a newer Airman Testing
   Community Advisory than the ones cited above, and for an updated
   *Frequently Missed ACS Codes* report.
2. Search current (last few months) test-prep blog posts and, if
   accessible, r/Part107 for newly reported recurring topics — never
   verbatim questions.
3. Add a new dated entry under the appropriate section above (do not
   delete older entries — this is a history).
4. Cross-check anything that sounds like a regulatory change against
   ecfr.gov (14 CFR Part 107) before treating it as settled.
5. If a claim conflicts with an entry already here, add a note rather than
   silently overwriting — future readers should be able to see that
   something changed.
